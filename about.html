About AgriCare

A phone app and soil sensors that help Suarez Farm catch crop diseases early and know what its soil needs.

Why we built it

At Suarez Farm in Pandi, Bulacan, crops and soil were checked by eye and by experience. We interviewed the owner, a farmer for over 20 years, and three problems stood out. Diseases were often noticed too late. The soil, clay in some parts and rocky in others, was never measured. And fertilizer was a guess: too much makes rice grow lush but bear little grain, and too little holds it back.

So we built AgriCare, an IoT-based mobile system for crop health monitoring using image recognition and soil quality analysis. Farmers take a photo of a rice, tomato, eggplant or chili leaf, and the AI says whether it is healthy or names the possible disease, with what to do. A sensor in each field sends the soil's moisture and NPK, so the app can tell when to water and which fertilizer to add. Weather advice comes from this week's forecast, and alerts warn of disease, poor soil and bad weather. There are three apps: Farmer for the farm workers, Admin for the owner and Developer for our team. They work in Filipino and English, as the owner asked.

We followed the Agile method, working in cycles of requirements, design, development, testing, deployment and review. After each round of feedback from our panel and evaluators, we improved the app. AgriCare aims to cut crop losses through early action, use water and fertilizer wisely, help farmers make better decisions, and give other farms nearby a model to follow.
